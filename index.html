Abstract

A surgical video model can recognize a maneuver by identifying the tool and its action. Following the maneuver also requires knowing when the instrument acts, how it moves, and where it touches tissue. Existing surgical benchmarks usually evaluate these questions separately. TACTUS brings them together within the same action.

We present TACTUS, a dataset of 22,995 action clips spanning 2.9 million frames from 141 videos across five public sources. Annotators identify the tool and action and mark the interaction window within the full action. Nearly all clips also include per-frame tool landmarks and tissue contact regions. In 15,668 Gold clips, people place the spatial prompts for tracking, then review and re-check the annotations. In 7,327 Silver clips, a model places the spatial prompts.

On the Gold test set, our reference model, TactusNet, identifies the tool and action together in 93.8% of clips after training on Gold. Adding Silver raises that accuracy to 95.5%. In a separate Gold-only experiment, fine-tuning an encoder for each head group reaches 95.8%. Pinpointing the interaction remains harder. ActionFormer recalls 46.6% of interaction windows at a temporal IoU threshold of 0.7, and SegFormer reaches a Dice score of 0.351 for overlap with the annotated contact region.

Method

TactusNet, the reference network

Stage I

Semantic Head

The instrument and action, decoded as one of the six valid tool–action pairs.

Stage II

Temporal Head

The start and end of the interaction window within the action window.

Stage III

Spatial Head

TactusNet first locates the tool on each full frame and crops around its landmarks. Separate decoders predict three tool landmarks and four contact-region corners in the crop. A final pass refines the tool landmarks at native resolution.

Stage IV

Track Propagation

For dense annotation, TactusNet selects the most confident frame within the interaction window separately for tool points and contact corners. CoTracker3 tracks each group through the full action window.
